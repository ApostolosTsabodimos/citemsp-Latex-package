\subsection*{1. Plain citations and range compression}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\noindent
\texttt{\textbackslash cite} baseline (5 consecutive): \cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4,ref5} \\
\texttt{\textbackslash citemsp} same keys:              \citemsp{ref1, ref2, ref3, ref4, ref5} \\[4pt]
Non-consecutive (\texttt{\textbackslash cite}):         \cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref5,ref6,ref7} \\
Non-consecutive (\texttt{\textbackslash citemsp}):      \citemsp{ref1, ref2, ref3, ref5, ref6, ref7} \\[4pt]
All ten:   \citemsp{ref1, ref2, ref3, ref4, ref5, ref6, ref7, ref8, ref9, ref10} \\
Single:    \citemsp{ref3}

% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\subsection*{9. Drop-in \texttt{\textbackslash cite} replacement}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\noindent
\texttt{\textbackslash citemsp} with no slashes behaves like \texttt{\textbackslash cite}:

\medskip\noindent
\verb|\cite{ref3}| $\longrightarrow$ \cite{ref3} \\
\verb|\citemsp{ref3}| $\longrightarrow$ \citemsp{ref3} \\[4pt]
\verb|\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4,ref5}| $\longrightarrow$ \cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4,ref5} \\
\verb|\citemsp{ref1, ref2, ref3, ref4, ref5}| $\longrightarrow$ \citemsp{ref1, ref2, ref3, ref4, ref5}

\subsection*{10. Realistic usage in running text}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Schwarzschild solution~\citemsp{ref2/1/3} was found shortly after the
field equations~\citemsp{ref1} were published. A modern treatment can be
found in standard textbooks~\citemsp{ref3/6.1/2, ref5/b21.1}, where the
derivation proceeds via the Birkhoff theorem~\citemsp{ref3/T6.2}. The
singularity theorems~\citemsp{ref6, ref4/1/e4.3} later showed that
gravitational collapse is generic. For further context
see~\citemsp{ref7, ref8, ref9, ref10}.
